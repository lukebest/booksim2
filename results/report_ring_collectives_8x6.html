Collectives on the 8×6 dimension-sliced bufferless ring

Two questions, one fabric. First: what makespan does the HPCA'22 mechanism (E-tag/I-tag plus deflection, unicast only) reach on the six collectives? Second: on the same physical ring, what can a static slot table do if ring stations may copy-and-continue and nodes may accumulate in L1?

Fabric: 6 row rings × 8 + 8 column rings × 6 = 192 directed segments over 48 nodes, every node a bridge, zero buffering inside a ring. Conflicts are the same D-R five-clause predicate used throughout this repository. All 56 executable checks pass.

At one port per ring station the ring loses the deep-pipeline case. R=1: ring by 0.885×, R=5: mesh by 1.006×, R=13: mesh by 1.190×. The ring's advantage at R=1 is a span advantage — its data spans half a mesh diameter. At R=13 the binding resource is the station's single insert/extract port, and the mesh's Hamilton bi-tree (II_eff = 1.0) pipelines past it. Only the 2-port ring wins at every R. Quoting the ring's win without naming the port count would be the single most misleading number in this report.
The ranking does not survive the pipeline depth. best MESH scheme changes with R: R=1:axis+CCW -> R=5:axis+CCW -> R=13:Hamilton bi-tree; at ring ports=1 the ring/mesh winner changes with R: R=1:ring(0.885), R=5:mesh(1.006), R=13:mesh(1.19). Quoting a single R would have picked a winner by accident.

Part 2 — four structural levers

Each lever came with a prediction. Two of the four came out differently than predicted, and those are the interesting ones.

Lever 1 — arc multicast (copy and continue)

reduce, gather, alltoall have no T1 row, and that is the point. Copy-and-continue is a fan-out primitive. A fan-in (reduce, gather) has nothing to replicate, and all-to-all's N(N−1) messages are all distinct, so no station can serve two of them with one copy. For these patterns the multicast hardware buys exactly nothing and the verification suite asserts T1 is byte-identical to T0.

Two different mechanisms hide in this column. On the multicast schemes the traffic ratio is 1.00× while the makespan ratio is 1.64×: a copy-and-continue arc drops the same copy at the same stations whichever way it goes round, so going both ways halves the span and moves no fewer flits. That is a latency win, not a bandwidth win. On the unicast schemes it is a bandwidth win too, but for the other reason: a clockwise-only route is simply longer, so it costs 1.74× the arc cycles (broadcast / dim_2phase / T0). The blanket claim that bidirectional routing "halves the peak arc load" holds for neither case as stated.

Lever 3 — full-ring rotation

A rotation step on an 8-node row ring. Rotation is the only scheme here that meets the busiest-segment bound exactly, and the same rigidity is what makes it the most fault-intolerant scheme in the set.

Lever 4 — L1 accumulation chain

Folding in L1 keeps every hop the same size as the payload, so the reduce tree boards 1.74× fewer flits than the gather tree that moves the same data (611 vs 1,066 at m=13), and finishes in 189 rather than 657 cycles.

For allreduce at m=13, reduce-then-broadcast on the dimension tree (T1) lands at 284 cycles on 793 boarded flits, against 309 cycles on 3,744 flits for recursive halving-doubling (T0). Note the ordering: halving-doubling boards 4.7× the traffic and is still the faster of the two T0 options, so on this fabric concurrency outranks total traffic.

Burst jitter on allgather / ring_rotate / T0 at m=13. The three curves are the three ways to react: shift everything, resynchronize per phase, or recompile with the late release times as constraints. Only the third can absorb anything, and what it absorbs is exactly the slack the packer left behind.
J* is a weak metric on a rigid schedule. Under a hard barrier the makespan grows by exactly the worst lateness, so J* is just 5% of the makespan restated — it says nothing about the schedule. The absorbed-cycles column is the informative one, because it measures slack that actually exists.

Results contrary to expectation

  • Rotation does not reach 100% link utilization. The plan predicted 1.0. Measured: 81.0% at R=47, rising monotonically but only asymptotically. It does hit the busiest-segment bound exactly (II_eff = 47.0 = per-round arc load), but the fill cost T1=564 never amortizes away: utilization is II·R/(T1+II·(R−1)), which approaches 1 only as R→∞.
  • A one-port ring loses to the mesh once the pipeline is deep. The ring wins the single-shot case (0.885× at R=1) on span alone, but at R=13 it is behind by 1.190×: the binding resource has moved from distance to the station's single insert/extract port, and the mesh's Hamilton bi-tree pipelines past it with II_eff = 1.0. The mesh's own best scheme changes at that depth too, so neither fabric can be ranked from one R.
  • A contiguous dead node does not cut the ring. The plan assumed one dead node equals two breaks, so a station bypass mux would be mandatory. On a 2-connected ring a contiguous hole is routable the long way round, and removing the bypass mux costs nothing for contiguous node and quadrant faults. It is scattered dead nodes on one ring that partition it, which is where the mux earns its area (9 extra infeasible scenarios without it across the measured schemes).
  • A dead node forces an extra phase, not just a reroute. A dimension-sliced algorithm hands a row's data to a column through the one node where they meet. Kill that node and the whole column loses that row — the fabric is still connected, but the schedule had exactly one path. 12 of 26 recompiles on allgather / dim_2phase / T1 need a repair phase appended. Reporting these as "recompiles with 1.2× inflation" would have hidden a missing phase.
  • Bidirectional routing halves the span, not the load — wherever multicast is doing the work. The plan predicted "peak arc load halved". On broadcast / dim_2phase / T1 the traffic ratio measures 1.00× against a makespan ratio of 1.64×: one arc drops copies at the same stations whichever way it travels, so only the span shrinks. The unicast schemes do save arc cycles, but because the one-way path is longer, not because the load was split.
  • The paper mechanism beats the static calendar on the funnel collectives. On reduce / dim_2phase / T0 at m=13, ring_base finishes in 107 cycles against the calendar's 189. This is a modelling difference, not deflection magic: the calendar reserves an extract port for a whole burst, while ring_base interleaves individual flits at the L1 drain rate. Widening the model to two extract ports moves the calendar to 61 (1.00×), which is the honest apples-to-apples comparison.
  • 3 of the verification suite's labelled predictions are recorded as refuted rather than quietly relaxed; see results/verify_ring_collectives_8x6.json.

Known limitations

  • The ring-versus-mesh comparison is "best point in each design space", and the two spaces are not the same shape: the mesh sweeps crossbar write width, drain rate and FIFO depth, the ring sweeps only station port count. That is why the table gives one row per ring port count instead of a single summary ratio.
  • The calendar model charges an extract port to one transfer for its whole burst (m·σ cycles). The paper mechanism drains L1 per flit, so the two are not on the same footing for funnel-shaped collectives; the port sensitivity table bounds the gap rather than closing it.
  • Reduction is modelled as an item-set union with a size-preserving fold. That captures traffic and dependency order exactly, but not arithmetic: an adder's latency inside L1 is folded into RAMP and not modelled separately.
  • ring_islip2d is included as a same-capability scheduling reference, not as a serious contender — it arbitrates one flit per node per round, so on 2256-message patterns its makespan is an order of magnitude off and should be read as a control, not a result.
  • Jitter is injected only at source release. In-flight jitter would need the transport model rather than the calendar replay.
  • Fault recompilation assumes an offline compiler with the full fault list. No claim is made about how long the recompile takes or how the new table is distributed.
  • σ=1 throughout the calendar work. The metal-constant σ=2 reading that the topology audit reports (1.1707× the mesh's wire) is not re-run here.
